LEGAL / RESPONSIBLE USE

A learning project, not a permission slip.

Nabda was created to develop software-engineering, user-experience, security, and maintenance skills. Anyone operating a copy remains responsible for how it is configured, who receives messages, what is sent, and whether the operation is lawful.

01

Purpose and project status

Nabda is a personal educational and maintenance project. Its primary purposes are learning programming, system design, API security, queue operations, Android integration, user experience, testing, and long-term software maintenance. It is not legal advice, a telecommunications licence, a carrier service, an emergency service, or a guarantee that any message will be delivered.

Simulation first. The distributed Simulation APK cannot send carrier SMS. Live operation requires a separate security, legal, carrier, and operational review.
02

Acceptable-use conditions

The software may be used only for expected transactional, authentication, or service messages to recipients for whom the operator can document an appropriate basis and consent context. The operator must provide a practical stop mechanism, respect opt-outs immediately, and comply with platform, carrier, and legal limits.

Prohibited uses

  • Unsolicited advertising, spam, harassment, threats, deception, or repeated contact after an opt-out.
  • Impersonation, fraud, forged sender identity, phishing, credential theft, or misleading authentication messages.
  • Unauthorized access, interception, surveillance, bypassing security controls, or using another person’s device, SIM, account, or data without authority.
  • Illegal content, intellectual-property infringement, discrimination, exploitation, or any conduct prohibited by applicable law.
  • Circumventing quotas, rate limits, blocklists, audit controls, carrier rules, or technical live-send safeguards.
03

Operator responsibility and misuse

The person or organization operating a Nabda installation bears responsibility for its API keys, devices, SIMs, recipients, message content, consent evidence, opt-out handling, carrier charges, staff access, infrastructure, backups, security configuration, and compliance. The project owner does not direct or approve third-party messages and, to the maximum extent permitted by applicable law, is not responsible for unlawful, abusive, negligent, or unauthorized use by an operator or user.

This allocation of responsibility is not an attempt to exclude liability that cannot legally be excluded. It does not protect the project owner, an operator, or any other person from responsibility for their own intentional acts, legally actionable negligence, statutory duties, or conduct that mandatory law places on them.

04

Recipient data and privacy notice

A Nabda installation may process recipient phone numbers, message content, message status, consent references, contact records, opt-outs, device identifiers, API-key metadata, webhook events, and audit facts. These data are processed to validate, queue, deliver or simulate, observe, secure, and audit expected transactional communications. Sensitive message fields are designed for automatic redaction under the configured retention period; opt-out records may be retained to prevent future contact.

For a self-hosted installation, the organization that decides why and how recipient data are entered is responsible for providing any required notice, establishing a lawful basis, limiting access, handling requests, securing its infrastructure, and defining any international transfer. The technical project does not replace that notice or decision.

05

Libyan-law reference points

These references are a compliance checklist, not a definitive legal opinion. Confirm the official text, amendments, implementing regulations, licensing position, and facts of the intended operation with a qualified Libyan lawyer before public or live use.

LAW 5 / 2022

Cybercrime and misuse

Articles 11–17 address unauthorized access, interception, harmful manipulation, and unwanted promotional messages; Article 17 specifically links unwanted promotion with giving recipients a way to stop it. Articles 47–50 address unlawful interception, management responsibility for a legal person, application of other penal laws, and confiscation or closure consequences.

LAW 6 / 2022

Electronic transactions and data

The private-data chapter includes express-consent rules for the entities named in Article 73, confidentiality duties in Article 75, a broad notice duty for a person controlling personal data in electronic transactions in Article 76, access and update provisions in Article 77, transfer safeguards in Article 78, and restrictions on harmful or repurposed processing in Article 79.

LAW 22 / 2010

Telecommunications licensing

Article 2 states a licensing requirement for establishing or administering telecommunications networks or providing communications services. Whether a particular deployment falls within that scope is a fact-specific regulatory question. Nabda documentation and a customer-owned SIM do not replace any required approval.
